`claude-opus-5`, ten finished sessions across four topics, twenty draws each,
replayed through `feedback.review_session` off committed cassettes.

Generated by `python -m evals.review.replay --repeat 20`; the tables below are
`evals/review/report.py` over `observations.json`.

| | A6.5 | A6.6 |

A6.5 measured the review and found one failure: `greetings/partner_name`,
recovered 20/20 as the final turn, 11/20 as the oldest, and **0/20**
mid-session. A6.6 changed nothing about the slot rules and nothing about the
rubric. It changed how the conversation is encoded in the request — from
replayed `user`/`assistant` messages to one numbered transcript in a single user
message — and all three positions went to 20/20.

The A6.5 hypothesis (the review collapses `self_name` and `partner_name` into
one already-credited event) is not supported: the same corpus, same labels,
same model recovers that slot at every draw once the grader is reading a record
instead of sitting inside a conversation. See the A6.6 section of
[`docs/streams/grading.md`](../../docs/streams/grading.md).

## What 100% does and does not mean

It is a rate over **this** corpus: ten sessions, four topics, at most four turns
each, twenty draws. It says the earlier-turn failure A6 and A6.5 both measured
is gone from every case built to catch it. It does not say the review is
correct on sessions the corpus does not contain — longer ones especially, where
the transcript is the thing that grows.

The corpus has also stopped discriminating: every case passes at every draw, so
it can now only report a regression, never localise a remaining defect. The next
useful thing to do with it is to make it harder — longer sessions, more slots
per turn, a session where two turns fill the same slot — not to read 100% as the
end of the question.

## What is asserted, and what is only reported

`tests/test_review_eval.py` gates two things off these cassettes and spends
nothing to do it:

- **Spurious credit, at every draw.** The pass is add-only, so a slot it invents
  reaches a card the learner reads as truth and cannot be taken back. That is
  not a rate.
- **Recall, as a floor — raised from 80% to 95% by A6.6.** Still under the
  measurement rather than at it: 100% is not a rate anyone can promise, the
  corpus is re-recorded weekly, and 95% leaves eleven misses of 220 for noise
  while failing on any real return of the earlier-turn bug. A floor left at 80%
  would have let the review fall the whole way back to A6.5 and stay green.

The per-slot table above is reported, not asserted.
